Every October the same questions come in: when do I need winter tires, which ones count, and is my all-season good enough? Here's the short version for drivers around Vancouver.

## The dates

On most designated highways in British Columbia, winter tires are required on passenger vehicles from **1 October to 30 April**. Some routes on the coast end earlier. The rule applies where the signs are posted, so the sign at the start of a route is what counts. Before a trip, check the route on DriveBC.

In the city itself there's no blanket requirement. But the Sea-to-Sky, the Coquihalla and the road to the ski hills all have the signs, and many Vancouver drivers use them every winter.

## What counts as a winter tire

Two markings on the sidewall pass in BC:

- **The mountain and snowflake symbol** (a three-peak mountain with a snowflake inside). The tire passed a standard snow traction test.
- **M+S** (mud and snow). Many all-season tires carry it.

Either way, the tread must be at least **3.5 mm** deep. A tire with the right marking and less tread doesn't count.

## Legal isn't the same as good

M+S is legal, but it isn't a snow test. Most all-season tires carry the mark and still harden in the cold. Winter compounds stay soft below about 7 °C, which is most of a Vancouver winter, wet roads included. If you drive to the mountains or start work before the roads are gritted, the mountain and snowflake symbol is worth it.

## A sensible routine

1. **Book the swap in late September.** October fills up fast.
2. **Measure the tread.** A coin gauge is fine; we measure every tire at the swap and write it on the work order.
3. **Check the pressure after the first cold morning.** Pressure drops about 1 psi for every 5 to 6 °C.
4. **Store the off-season set** clean, dry, away from sunlight, and stacked flat or hung on rims.
5. **Swap back in April or May**, once nights stay above about 7 °C, unless you're still heading into the mountains before 30 April.

## Quick answers

- *Do I need four winter tires?* Yes. Two on one axle makes the car behave unpredictably.
- *All-weather tires?* Many carry the mountain and snowflake symbol and pass. They're a fair choice if you'd rather not swap twice a year.
- *What's the fine?* It depends on the offence and can change. Police can also turn you back at the sign.
