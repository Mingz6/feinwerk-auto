A 2014 BMW 328i came in on a Tuesday with the engine light on and a shake at idle. The owner had already been to another shop twice. The first visit replaced one ignition coil. The second replaced the other three. The shake came back within a week each time.

This is how we found the real cause, and why it took us under two hours.

## What the codes said

The scan showed misfires on more than one cylinder, plus a lean code: the engine computer was adding fuel because it measured too much oxygen in the exhaust. A coil that fails usually misfires one cylinder, the same one, every time. Misfires that move around, together with a lean code, point somewhere else: air getting into the engine that the airflow sensor never measured.

A misfire code tells you where the symptom is. It doesn't tell you why.

## Fuel trims before parts

Before touching anything, we watched the fuel trims, the percentage the computer adds or removes to keep the mixture right. At idle the long-term trim sat at about +18%. At 2,500 rpm it dropped to about +4%.

That pattern matters. A small leak lets in a fixed amount of extra air. At idle, when the engine breathes very little, that leak is a big share of the total, so the computer adds a lot of fuel. At higher rpm the same leak is a tiny share, so the correction almost disappears. A weak fuel pump or a dirty injector does the opposite: it gets worse under load.

## The smoke test

We capped the intake and fed it low-pressure smoke from a smoke machine. Within a minute a thin line of smoke came out from under the intake manifold. A small vacuum hose there had cracked along its underside, where you can't see it from above. With the engine running, the crack opened and closed with the engine's vibration, which is why the misfire came and went.

## The fix

One hose, about half an hour of labour, then we cleared the fuel adaptations and drove the car for twenty minutes. Idle trims settled within about 3% of zero, and the misfires stopped. We sent the owner a photo of the crack next to the new hose before we fitted it, as we do with every line on a work order.

The four coils weren't wasted, exactly: they were near the end of their life. But they weren't the problem.

## What to take from it

- If a misfire comes back after new coils or plugs, ask whether anyone has looked at fuel trims.
- Misfires on several cylinders plus a lean code often mean unmetered air. A smoke test finds most leaks in minutes.
- Ask for a photo of the failed part. A shop that found the cause can show it to you.
